These days, when you hear about AI, the term AGI even comes up.Even today’s AI already does a large part of the work that humans used to do, but if AGI, which is truly human-like or possesses even greater general intelligence, were to emerge, it probably wouldn’t just be a matter of a new technology being created.I believe that the very structure of society, which humans have taken for granted until now, may actually change.Today’s society is fundamentally structured so that people work, receive money in return, and live off that money.But if AGI can perform most knowledge work, and robots are added to the mix, then the story changes.A being arises that can work far faster and more cheaply than humans, eliminating the need to demand the same labor from everyone.A somewhat strange problem arises from here.Production can happen even if people do not work, so then how should people make money?Until now, if you had ability, you could turn that ability into labor and make money.A programmer creates programs, a doctor provides medical treatment, a lawyer offers legal services, and a businessman moves people and capital to create new value.But if AGI can do all of this far better than humans, then human ability itself would no longer be scarce.In the end, I think what matters is not the ability itself, but the position in which that ability can be put to use.For example, a person who uses one AI and a person who runs a company using thousands of AIs will have completely different levels of influence even if they live in the same AI era.So, in the AGI era, I think it may become more important than differences in human intelligence to how much AI can be utilized, what resources it possesses, and what goals it can set.From this perspective, there is a possibility that the status of humans may also change somewhat in the future, unlike now.It may not be simply those who study well or work well who rise to high positions, but rather those who create something by effectively leveraging AI, those who own AI and capital, and those who connect AI with multiple people to drive an organization—people who generate influence may hold greater value.Then, in the end, what should humans do?I don’t think this is a problem that ends simply with “We need to learn AI so we don’t lose jobs to it.”No matter how well AI is used, someone will emerge who uses AI better than AI, and someone with even more capital and resources than that person will emerge.If so, we need to consider what ultimately remains at the end of competition.Perhaps the most important thing is not “what you can do,” but “what you want to do.”Because if AI becomes better at doing work than humans, there is no need for humans to compete to do the same work as AI.What remains for humans may be setting goals, judging what is important, building relationships with others, and choosing the direction of life they want.Of course, this will also depend on how far AGI develops.If AGI merely assists human tasks, the current social structure may not change significantly.But if AGI develops to a level where it possesses intelligence far superior to that of humans and can plan and carry out its own actions, then the story changes completely from that point on.At that time, rather than the question of “Which jobs will survive?” it seems the more important question will be “How can humans be recognized as valuable in society without having to work?”After all, money is also a means of exchanging value created by humans, and if AI takes on most of the production, we may need to rethink the very standards by which money is paid to humans.It could be basic income, a way for society as a whole to share the benefits generated by AI or robots, or it could lead to the creation of a completely new economic system entirely different from the one we have now.We still don't know what form it will take.But I think there is one thing that is quite certain.If AGI were to develop to a level that truly surpasses human intelligence, the way humans live would no longer be the same as it is now.And I think the most important issue in that change is not whether AI will replace humans, but what will be used to judge human value after AI has replaced human labor.Perhaps the most difficult problem in the AGI era is not how to make a living, but what humans will live for even after the problem of making a living has been solved.
